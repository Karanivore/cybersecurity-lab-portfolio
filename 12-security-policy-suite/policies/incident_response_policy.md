# Incident Response Policy

**Document owner:** CISO | **Version:** 1.0 | **Effective:** 2026-08-01
**Review cycle:** Annual | **Classification:** Internal
**ISO 27001:2022 references:** A.5.24, A.5.25, A.5.26, A.5.27, A.5.28, A.6.8
**Framework alignment:** NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 2

## 1. Purpose

To establish the requirement for a consistent, documented approach to
detecting, reporting, assessing, responding to, and learning from
information security incidents.

## 2. Scope

All security events and incidents affecting organizational information,
systems, or third-party services processing organizational data.

## 3. Policy Statements

1. **Reporting duty.** All personnel must report suspected security
   events promptly through the designated channel (24/7 contact).
2. **Defined lifecycle.** Incidents are managed through Preparation →
   Identification → Containment → Eradication → Recovery → Lessons
   Learned (NIST SP 800-61). Operational detail is maintained in
   incident response playbooks (e.g. the ransomware playbook).
3. **Severity and escalation.** Incidents are triaged and assigned a
   severity that drives escalation timelines and stakeholder
   notification.
4. **Roles.** An Incident Commander leads each declared incident;
   supporting roles (SOC, IT Ops, Legal, Communications) are defined in
   the playbooks.
5. **Evidence handling.** Evidence is preserved to support investigation
   and potential legal action; systems are isolated (not powered off)
   where forensics may be required.
6. **Breach notification.** Where an incident constitutes a reportable
   breach, notification to regulators, customers, and affected
   individuals occurs within the timeframes required by applicable law
   and contract, coordinated with Legal.
7. **Post-incident review.** A blameless after-action review is completed
   for significant incidents, feeding corrective actions and detection
   improvements.

## 4. Testing and Exercising

Incident response capability is exercised at least annually (e.g.
tabletop or simulation), and playbooks are updated based on findings and
real incidents.

## 5. Metrics

The program tracks time-to-detect, time-to-contain, and time-to-recover,
reported to leadership as part of security governance.

## 6. Compliance

Failure to report or cooperate with incident response is a policy
violation handled under the Information Security Policy.
